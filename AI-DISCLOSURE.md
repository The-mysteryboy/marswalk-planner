# AI assistance and authorship

Marswalk Planner was conceived, directed and decided by its author, and built with AI coding assistance (Claude, by Anthropic, used through Claude Code).

## What the author did
- Chose the challenge (Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map) and set the goal: give a future astronaut a clear GO, CAUTION or NO-GO answer for a Marswalk, with the reasons.
- Decided the scope: which features to build and which to cut, including the route report, Play the walk, the turn-back point, Reachable science, the rover path check, and the science stops along the route.
- Set the standards the project is held to: every number sourced or labelled as an assumption, no invented data, no claim of live weather, and honest statements of limits.
- Tested the app on a real phone, found problems (a landscape scroll trap, no way to reset results, overlapping map messages, slow base maps, hidden tools), and directed the fixes.
- Reviewed the results and made the final decisions: the open-source licence, the public repository, and the submission materials.
- Recorded the demo voice-over.

## What AI assistance did
- Wrote and tested much of the code, the build scripts and the automated tests, and wrote the documentation.
- Researched the data sources and licence terms, and drafted the project text, all under the author's direction.
- Captured and assembled the demo footage with scripts that record the real app on screen, under the author's direction.

## What AI did not do
- No AI-generated images, video or audio appear in the demo. The demo is a real screen recording of the app with added labels, a pointer, a zoom and subtitles.
- No data values were invented or generated by AI. Derived data, such as the 40 m elevation grid, was computed by scripts from the sources listed in SOURCES.md.

The app and its tests are open source under Apache-2.0.
