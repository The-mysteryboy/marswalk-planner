          About Marswalk Planner

          A map of Jezero Crater that layers data from several NASA missions. Place a habitat, draw a route, and get a Go, Caution or No-Go report with the reasons, plus the science stops along the way. Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge "Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map".

          Press Why? or any ? beside a number to see how it was worked out for your route. Switch the map to Check a point to read any spot. Example missions are ready-made walks. More tools holds the safest route search, assumption presets, slope map and map layers, and each layer has a What am I looking at? card.

          How this answers the challenge

          • A layered, integrated view: map layers from several missions on one map, and Check a point to read elevation and slope anywhere.
          • Multiple NASA missions: Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, Mars Global Surveyor, Mars Odyssey, Perseverance and Curiosity, with Mars Express (ESA) elevation.
          • A route and destinations: the route report, the nine science stops, and Reachable science for which stops a walk from base can reach.
          • Conditions: published measurements with where and when each was taken, and the latest Curiosity weather feed reading with its age in days.
          • Data to complete a walk safely: a GO, CAUTION or NO-GO verdict with its reasons, the time out and back, and the turn-back point.
          • Science along the way: the stops near the route, why each matters, and its source.

          The limits: every slope limit, speed and time budget is this project's assumption, not NASA's.

          Missions and data

          • Mars Global Surveyor, MOLA: global laser altimeter elevation shading.
          • Mars Express (ESA), HRSC: stereo elevation blended with MOLA at 200 m.
          • Mars Odyssey, THEMIS: day and night infrared mosaics.
          • Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter: CTX imagery and the CTX elevation model behind every slope, HiRISE imagery, and CRISM mineral detections.
          • Mars 2020 Perseverance: the rover's real traverse, its sample sites, and MEDA weather data.
          • Mars Science Laboratory Curiosity, RAD: the surface radiation measurement.

          Map tiles come from NASA Mars Trek. Elevation comes from the USGS Mars 2020 CTX DEM Mosaic. Every value is listed in SOURCES.md.

          How the elevation was checked

          The 40 m report grid, averaged from the 20 m CTX elevation model, was compared with 708 points on Perseverance's route (about 3 m typical difference, 3.1 m RMS). The sources do not state how the rover elevations were produced, so this is a comparison, not an independent measurement.

          Assumptions

          These are planning placeholders chosen for this tool. None is a NASA requirement, and all can be edited under Assumptions.

          • Walking speed of 3.5 km/h on flat ground.
          • Slope changes speed following the Tobler hiking function, which was fitted to people walking on Earth, not to suited astronauts on Mars.
          • An 8 hour EVA budget with 60 minutes of science time.
          • Caution above 15° of slope or under 25% budget margin. No-Go above 25° of slope, over budget, or off the elevation data.
          • The walk returns along the same route.

          The Cautious and Ambitious presets are this project's own assumptions, not NASA values or a NASA standard. Standard is the set above, and it is used unless a link names another preset.

          Known limits

          • Slopes are averaged over about 50 m, so short steep steps, boulders and small craters do not show. A Go here is not proof that the ground is walkable.
          • The tool has no model of suit limits, loose ground, lighting, or dust storms.
          • Walking speed has no fatigue, suit mass or Mars-gravity effect. It changes only with slope.
          • Conditions are published snapshots, not a forecast. Radiation was measured at Gale Crater, not Jezero.
          • Science stops use the rover's last recorded position before each sample was sealed, so they mark the area rather than the exact outcrop.
